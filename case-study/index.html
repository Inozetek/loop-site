The product

Plans with friends, without the group chat stall

Loop combines your calendar, your location, your taste and the people you like spending time with, then suggests the right place or event at the right time. The north star says what it's for: group plans completed each week where at least two people actually showed up.

Patent pending

Not just what's good, but when you can realistically go

For every pick, Loop works out the soonest time you could actually arrive. It combines your free time, the venue's real hours (including places open past midnight) and how long it takes to get there, and it keeps that time current as the day goes on. Early builds suggested a venue 32 miles away for 22 minutes from now. Fixing cases like that became the basis of Loop's patent-pending method.

Hard problems

The decisions behind the product

Keeping AI and API costs flat as usage grows

A nightly job caches places and events from several providers (Yelp, Foursquare, SeatGeek, Ticketmaster) into Postgres with PostGIS, so the app reads its own data instead of paying per tap. On top of that: per-API daily budgets, per-user AI quotas, a billing-safe mode, and a refresh job designed to stay inside free tiers.

Recommendations that explain themselves honestly

A review caught "why this fits" reasons that claimed personalization without evidence, and a small friend signal outranking a much stronger match. Now a reason only appears when real evidence backs it, reasons sort by actual weight, and a test pins that behavior.

Measuring AI quality instead of eyeballing it

Evaluation scripts score the feed: a quality gauntlet across categories, an LLM judge that rates the relevance of the top five picks, and a readiness check per city. Engine changes have to hold those scores.

Fixing what users see first

In August, 75% of feed cards showed a placeholder instead of a photo. A reproducible probe traced most failures to one image host rejecting stored URLs, and placeholders fell to 55% within two weeks.

Keeping keys off the phone

AI and maps calls are moving behind server-side proxies on Supabase Edge Functions, with per-user and global caps, so paid API keys stay off the device.

How it was built

A product team of one

Loop was built like a small product team: one person owning product, design, architecture and final review, with AI coding tools handling much of the implementation. A few habits kept quality high as the codebase grew.

  • Independent review. No change merged without a separate check against written acceptance criteria.
  • Living documentation. Design docs, a launch tracker and a findings log kept everything current. The rule: every number in a doc comes with the command that reproduces it.
  • Real-device testing. Every build tested on a real phone before it moved forward.
